INTERPENETRATION 
Interpenetration allows some troops to pass through 
others. An interpenetration is allowed during a 
movement, a charge or an evade move. 
Permitted interpenetrations 
The only permitted interpenetrations are: 
* 
Non-included commanders can pass through 
and be passed through by all friendly troops in 
any direction. 
½ 
Light infantry can pass through all other friendly 
troops in any direction. 
╗ 
All troops can pass through friendly light 
infantry in any direction. 
* 
Light cavalry can pass through other friendly 
mounted troops, provided they are oriented in 
the same or opposite direction. 
* 
All mounted troops can pass through friendly 
light cavalry, provided they are oriented in the 
same or opposite direction, 
* 
LMI can pass through friendly LMI, provided 
they are oriented in the same or opposite 
direction. 
* 
Knights on foot, and heavy or medium 
swordsmen that are non-Inppetuous, can pass 
through friendly bowmen, crossbowmen or 
handgunners (and vice versa) provided they are 
oriented in the same or opposite direction. This 
represents the ability of swordsmen to fight in 
coordination with shooter units. Mixed units 
cannot interpenetrate or be interpenetrated in 
this manner. 
* 
Foot units can pass through friendly artillery in 
any direction or through friendly war wagons by 
their long edge (i.e. across the narrowest part). 
Special cases 
* 
Interpenetration is not allowed if the friendly 
unit to be passed through is in melee or is 
providing support to a friendly unit in melee. 
* 
Mounted units cannot interpenetrate mounted 
that cause them panic. If mounted units are 
interpenetrated by other mounted that cause 
panic they lose one cohesion point. 
* 
Units of an unreliable allied corps cannot be 
interpenetrated. 
* 
A friendly or enemy camp can never be passed 
through. 
* 
If units in ambush are interpenetrated the 
ambush is immediately revealed.

General principles 
A unit that comes into contact with a new enemy, 
even with a single corner contact, engages in combat 
with that enemy. The unit must then conform (see p 
50). Contact can take place in the following cases: 
* 
During a charge (see p 42). 
╗ 
When moving to contact an enemy already in 
melee to support a friendly unit (see p 60). 
* 
During a pursuit after combat (see p 69). 
Sliding along the enemy

pursuing or is providing support. 
* 
The sliding unit is already in contact with this 
enemy at the beginning of its move and either: is 
no longer in contact at the end of its movement, 
charges, contacts another enemy, or pursues.

Rear contact: A unit can contact the rear edge of

CHARGE 
Definition 
A charge is 
a move in which a unit engages in 
combat with an enemy. The units must contact the 
enemy with part of its front edge (even a single 
corner). The charge stops once contact with the target 
is made. The charging unit or group must then 
conform (see p 50). A charge allows units to claim 
those bonuses that are only valid during the first 
round of a melee. 
There are three types of charges: 
* 
Commanded: This is a normal charge, in which 
the commander spends CPs. 
* 
Spontaneous: Carried out against enemies in the 
charging unit's ZOC, without expending CPs 
(see p 44). 
= 
Uncontrolled: When Impetuous units charge 
without orders (see p 45). 
Special cases 
* 
Acorner-to-corner contact is sufficient only if the 
unit can then conform at least partially. 
* 
If units are already in contact (even by a single 
corner), they cannot charge each other, but they 
can conform to engage in melee (see p 50). 
* 
Contacting an enemy already in melee to 
support a friendly unit is net a charge but a 
movement. 
Contact restrictions 
Most troops can freely contact enemy units. Some 
troops however were not primarily intended to 
engage in combat, and such units can only contact 
enemy units under certain conditions: 
Light infantry 
* 
In open terrain light infantry can only charge an 
enemy if, after conformation, they would be in 
melee with light infantry, light cavalry, elephant, 
scythed chariot, artillery or the enemy camp or 
they would be in a support position. 
* 
In rough or difficult terrain light infantry may 
charge any enemy who is either; penalised in 
combat in that terrain, is in Disorder, or to contact 
them on their flank or rear. If the enemy being 
charged is a heavy troop which would destroy LI 
in open terrain (see p 62) the entire front edge of 
the LI must end in the terrain after conformation 
for the charge to be allowed. 
* 
If, during a charge move, a light infantry would 
enter in melee with a unit it is not allowed to, the 
charge move stops just before (i.e. a few 
millimeters) contact. 
* 
Light infantry can always contact enemy (even 
heavy troops) already in melee to provide simple 
or melee support. 
Other units 
*╗ 
War wagons (except those with blades), artillery 
and Expendable levies can never charge or move 
into contact with enemy to provide a support. 
Charge range 
A unit is within charge range if both of the following 
conditions are met: 
╗ 
The distance between the two closest points of 
the units is less than or equal to the movement 
allowance of the charging unit. 
* 
The charging unit can make a charge movement 
(see point 4 p 42) that will contact the target unit: 
o 
If the movement includes a wheel, 4 or 2 
turn, the movement allowance of the unit is 
reduced accordingly. 
o 
If the movement includes a slide, the

Additional points 
" 
It is permitted to measure distances before 
charging to check if an enemy is within range. 
*" 
A group can charge if at least one unit of the 
group is within charge range of the enemy. 
However, only those units of the group that start 
within charge range will be able to conform with 
the enemy (see p 50). 
* 
A charge can take place on the enemy's front, 
flank or rear depending on the initial position of 
the charging unit (see p 41).
